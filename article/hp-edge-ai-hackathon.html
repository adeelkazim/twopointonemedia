HP Edge AI Hackathon: From Campus to Next-Day Highlight Film

Event Production November 5, 2026

On September 26, 2026, HP and San Jose State University brought student teams together in San Jose, California, for the Edge AI Hackathon. Adeel Kazim of Two Point One Media covered the event for agency energy UK, with HP as the end brand. The creative challenge was to make a technical event feel like a story about people, ideas, and the work of bringing those ideas to life.

That meant thinking beyond a collection of busy rooms and presentation slides. The pre-production brief set out a narrative that would begin with a quiet campus, build through student presentations and a lab tour, and finish with recognition, awards, and the group photo. The next-day highlight film below is the featured finished piece from that coverage.

Watch the Next-Day Highlight Film

Planning a Story, Not Just a Shot List

The brief treated the shape of the day as the foundation of the edit. Campus and hallway B-roll would establish the setting before the pace increased with arrivals and presentation rounds. The physical AI and digital twin lab tour was identified as a priority: a chance to connect the ideas being discussed with the spaces and technology around them.

Student interviews and judge soundbites were planned to give the coverage a human voice. Rather than relying only on what a camera could show from the back of a room, those conversations offered a way to explain what participants were building and why it mattered. Keynotes, finalist presentations, and awards would provide the closing movement.

A Solo Shoot with a Two-Camera Plan

Adeel's production approach paired handheld and gimbal coverage with a second camera locked off on the keynote stage. The plan was to keep a continuous stage recording while leaving him free to move for interviews, audience reactions, and B-roll. Two cameras, a gimbal, a tripod, wireless lavalier microphones, and a shotgun microphone formed the planned kit.

For a solo operator, those choices are connected. A fixed stage angle protects continuity; the moving camera provides the details and changes of perspective that an event story needs. Planning both roles before the event helps keep the coverage focused without treating every moment as the same kind of shot.

Shaping the Next-Day Edit

The finished highlight film brings together voices from the event, student demonstrations, presentation rooms, stage moments, and the group celebration. It moves between people and technology rather than staying on slides alone. That balance gives the audience a sense of the setting and the work, while keeping the participants at the centre of the story.

The pre-production scope also called for a teaser, short-form social clips, campus B-roll, edited interviews, full keynote recordings, and judge soundbites, including a same-day clip target. Those were planned deliverables, not a claim that every item was published or that the same-day target was met. This case study features the completed next-day highlight film.

Event Coverage Built Around the Edit

The HP x SJSU project shows why event filmmaking starts before the first camera is switched on. A clear narrative, defined camera roles, and attention to both voices and visual detail give an edit a stronger foundation than a shot list alone.
